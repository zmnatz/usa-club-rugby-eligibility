# How the eligibility report is worked out

This page explains how the [eligibility report](/) reaches each verdict: the rules, the data, how the data stays current, and how to check any result yourself.

**The report is a guide.** The Rugby Xplorer Match Participation Report remains the official count. The report doesn't check registration, because the match data has no registration details or USA IDs. It is not an official USA Rugby or USA Club Rugby tool.

## Checking a result

1. Open the [eligibility report](/) and choose a club. Each club has its own page at `/<club-key>`, so a verdict can be linked to.
2. Choose a player. Their detail opens over the report and lists every round of each side's season, what the player did in it, and whether it counted. It links to the player's own page, to keep or share.
3. Follow the Rugby Xplorer link on a round to compare it with the match report.

The number of rounds marked as counted is the player's Match Participation for that side.

**Reporting a problem.** All data comes from Rugby Xplorer. If your club's information looks wrong (missing matches, lineups or substitutions, or wrong team names or divisions), check and correct it in Rugby Xplorer first. The site picks up corrections on its next nightly update. If Rugby Xplorer is right and this site is still wrong, <a href={ISSUES_URL} target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">open an issue on GitHub</a>.

A past season is a Locked Season, so correcting Rugby Xplorer won't change its verdicts: they come from that season's rules and the team matching its pages describe. Its pages have two links instead, each opening an issue filled in with the season and club. Use **Report a verdict** when the Match Record is right but a verdict looks wrong under those rules (a team in the wrong division or club, two names that should be one club, or a player marked Waiver Required who didn't move). Use **Report a record correction** when the season's Match Record in the Archive is itself wrong, or was corrected in Rugby Xplorer after the season locked (a missing match, lineup or substitution), so the archive needs re-reading or correcting.

## The rules applied

These come from the [USA Club Rugby Senior Club Regulations 2026-27](https://usaclub.rugby/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/2026-27-Regulations.pdf), §3.4.2 and §3.5. The same rules apply to every club, by how many sides it fields.

**What counts as a match.** Match Participation is the number of NCS Qualifying Matches a player entered, as a starter or as a substitute who came on (Reg 3.4.2(e)).

- **Counts:** every senior club match in the fixture lists, unless its competition is excluded below. This is meant to be league and local playoff matches, and matches approved under Reg 3.4.3 where a union lists them as a competition.
- **Doesn't count:** competitions whose name marks them as Super Regionals, National Championships, 7s, friendlies, crossovers, socials or festivals.
- **Doesn't count:** American Rugby Premiership matches, which this report doesn't cover. ARP sides aren't assessed.
- **Doesn't count:** Men's Division IV and Women's Division III matches, because those divisions aren't part of the National Championship Series (Terms and Definitions, 34). Those sides aren't assessed, and a club with only those sides isn't listed.
- **Forfeits (Reg 3.4(h)):** a forfeit counts for every player on the non-forfeiting side's roster, starters and replacements, provided the roster was entered. It counts for no one on the forfeiting side. A forfeit whose winning side entered no roster is flagged with the gaps, because it then counts for nobody.
- **Doesn't count:** being listed as a substitute without coming on.
- **Doesn't count:** any match in June or July, the 7s season, whatever its competition is called. A team whose only matches were then isn't listed. Super Regionals and Nationals that reach June (2023's XVs Nationals ran 2 to 4 June) are 15s, so they decide which club a player last played for, but like every Super Regional and Nationals match they don't add to anyone's count. A Match Participation Report has no dates, so a CSV report is taken as given.

**A club with one side.** At least two NCS Qualifying Matches with the club (Reg 3.4.2(e)). A club counts as one-side when only one of its sides appears in the data.

**The top side of a club with several sides.** At least two NCS Qualifying Matches at any level with the club (Reg 3.5.2(b)).

**Any lower side.** All three of these must hold, where "this side or lower" includes every side below it:

- At least two matches for this side or lower (Reg 3.5.2(a)).
- Fewer than four matches for each side above, because a fourth match for any upper side forfeits eligibility for the sides below it (Reg 3.5(d)(i)).
- At least as many matches for this side or lower as for all the sides above combined (Reg 3.5(d)(ii)).

For example, at a club with divisions 1, 2 and 3, a player with two matches in each is eligible for divisions 1 and 2, but not 3. These rules give the same answer as all 20 examples in the regulations' Figure 1, which are part of the report's automated tests.

**On track (experimental).** Future playoff matches aren't always entered in Rugby Xplorer, so the possible fixtures may be incomplete. Players must be able to reach the two-match minimum by the start of the National Championship Series (Reg 3.5.1(a)), so a player passes if enough possible matches remain. These players appear as "on track" rather than "eligible", and their page lists the possible fixtures behind that. This means they could still qualify, not that they will: remaining fixtures aren't checked against availability, and they include playoff dates the side may not reach. Remaining fixtures never decide whether a player passes the four-match limit or the comparison with upper sides.

**Caution (experimental).** A player who is eligible for a lower side appears as "caution" when one more match for an upper side would end that eligibility for the rest of the season. That means either three matches for any one upper side, because a fourth forfeits the side (Reg 3.5(d)(i)), or being level with the upper sides' total with no fixtures left at or below this side to catch up (Reg 3.5(d)(ii)). A level player with fixtures remaining isn't flagged, because they can recover by playing them. Caution is a warning and doesn't change a verdict: these players still count as eligible. Check the official Rugby Xplorer record for matches not counted here, because a missing match or an unlisted fixture could change a caution or the verdict.

**Interpretations to review:**

- Forfeits: the side with the higher score is taken as the non-forfeiting side. The 48-hour deadline for locking the roster isn't visible in the data, so a roster entered late still counts. Replacements on the roster count as well as starters.
- The four-match limit applies to each upper side separately, not to their total. Figure 1's row with 3, 4 and 1 matches, eligible for divisions 1 and 2 only, requires this.
- The two-match minimum must be reachable before Super Regionals, which we take as the start of the National Championship Series.
- Competitions are excluded by name, so a new kind of competition counts until it is added to the list.
- A side's division comes from the end of its team name, such as "MD2", "W+D3" or "D2 1st XV". A team name without one, such as a festival or social side, can't be ranked and isn't assessed.
- Where a club has two sides at one level ("MD3" and "MD3 2nd XV"), the 1st XV is treated as the upper side.

**Not checked:**

- Registration, including the April 1 deadline, residency and international clearance (Reg 3.4(a) to (d)).
- Playing for another club in the same season (Reg 3.4(e)).
- Recognized Competitive Matches not entered as a competition in Rugby Xplorer (Reg 3.4.3).
- Waivers.

## Past seasons

Each season from 2022-23 is shown with the regulations of that year. The season is over, so a player is eligible or not, and there is no on track or caution. Rugby Xplorer holds nothing earlier.

| Season | A lower side is lost by | Half a player's matches with upper sides |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2025-26 | A fourth match for any one upper side | Keeps the lower side, if level |
| 2023-24, 2024-25 | Reaching 66% of the upper side's schedule, adding the upper levels together | Loses the lower side |
| 2022-23 | Reaching 66% of the schedule on any one upper side | Loses the lower side |

Every season needs two matches at the side's level or lower, or two at any level for the top side.

**Players who changed club.** A player with matches for more than one club in a past season appears only at the club they last played for, marked "Waiver Required". Their games for the other clubs are listed in their detail but don't count there: each club's verdict counts its own matches. Whether the move itself was allowed isn't checked.

**Interpretations to review for past seasons:**

- The regulations fix the 66% mark from the league schedule on the first weekend of play, which Rugby Xplorer doesn't keep. We use the final fixture list: every qualifying fixture for the side, played or not, except postponed ones. A side with two upper sides is measured against the one with fewer matches, as the regulations say.
- What counts as a match, the competitions excluded and the divisions assessed are today's in every season, since the record of past changes covers divisional eligibility only. 2025-26 uses the 2026-27 regulations.
- The comparison of half a player's matches adds their upper sides together, in every season.
- 66% is rounded to the nearest match, which matches each year's Figure 1, apart from the five-match schedule printed as 4 in 2023-24 and 2024-25. 2022-23 doesn't print the figure in the record we have, so it uses 2021-22's.
- Team names often had no division then, so a team takes the division named by the competitions of most of its fixtures, and its club from the rest of its name. A 2nd XV or B side with no division is taken as one division below its club's lowest side, or Division 3 if the club has no other side. A 1st and 2nd XV with no division between them are ranked in that order. Age tags such as "18+" mark a senior team, and the Rocky Mountain union's 2022-23 tiers count as divisions: Premiership as Division 2 and Championship as Division 3. American Rugby Premiership sides, playoff placeholders, 7s, 10s, selects and all-star sides aren't assessed. Any other team with no division that is alone at its club is treated as a one-side club, which needs only the two-match minimum. Championship rounds and one-off entries aren't assessed, nor is a team with no division at a club with other teams; those are listed on the season's page, and named on the club's page if it is published, because a missing upper side can make players below it look eligible. A club with two names at one division is left out, as they can't be ranked.

## How we get data from Rugby Xplorer

Everything entered in Rugby Xplorer is stored in its content management system (CMS), which has a public GraphQL API at `https://rugby-au-cms.graphcdn.app`. We read the same data entered for Rugby Xplorer rather than retyping it, and we don't copy anything from the xplorer.rugby website. We know of no published documentation for the API, so it could change without notice.

**The API can't return a player's history.** It can only return fixture lists and the details of individual matches. So every player's participation is worked out by us:

1. We load the fixture lists.
2. We load the lineup, substitutes and timeline of every played match. Each match is its own query, though one request can carry up to 100 of them.
3. We go through those matches and count each player's participation.

**No one list has every fixture.** The [Senior Club Council's list](https://xplorer.rugby/senior-club-council/fixtures-results?team=All&comp=All&season=All&tab=Fixtures) is the root for club teams, but it misses fixtures that its unions list, and a union's list misses some of its own fixtures, which appear only on the lists of the clubs playing them. So we read the council's list, every geographical union's list, and then each playing club's own list. A club's list also holds college, 7s and other matches, so from it we keep only the competitions the council and unions list. Clubs are found from the match records, which name each side's club. The home page lists a club once it has a fixture entered this season, played or not, so a club whose season starts later in the year isn't listed until its first fixture is entered.

**What we request:**

- **Fixture lists:** `getEntityFixturesAndResults`, 100 fixtures at a time, for the council, each union and each playing club, with up to 20 of those lists in one request.
- **Match details:** `getFixtureItem` for the score and clubs, `allMatchStatsSummary` for the lineup, substitutes and coaches, and `allMatchCommentary` for the timeline. Each request names the match by its competition, season and fixture id.

The API allows each address about 500 requests, refilling at about 4 a second, and reports what's left with every answer. We ask for up to 100 matches in one request, slow down when the allowance runs low, and wait when the API asks us to. We keep every match we've loaded, so a season before the current one is downloaded once and then kept as it is.

## The data used

- **Fixture lists:** every senior club fixture this season in the lists above.
- **Match details:** for each match, the score, lineup, listed substitutes and timeline of events.
- **Player identity:** the CMS player id, which stays the same for a player across matches. It links a player's appearances across a club's sides.

**How a substitute is counted.** Lineups list substitutes whether or not they played, so a substitute counts only if a substitution event in the match timeline names them coming on. A different spelling is accepted only when the surname matches exactly one listed substitute. Coaches listed on a team sheet aren't counted.

**Players with the same name.** Each CMS player id is treated as one player, because a shared name doesn't prove two records are one person. Where two players at a club share a name, the report lists them with each one's CMS id. If they are the same person with two Rugby Xplorer profiles, their matches stay split until the profiles are merged there, so each count may be low.

**Hidden profiles.** The CMS shows a player whose profile isn't public as "Name Withheld". Profiles should be public for qualifying matches and must be for playoffs and the National Championship Series (Reg 3.2(i), 3.4.1(i), 3.4.2(d)), although a player can hold an approved exemption. We can't tell who a hidden profile belongs to, so it isn't included in the report. A player with an exemption won't appear, so their club needs to check them separately.

**Known gaps.** The report flags two problems beside the verdicts, because either can make a count too low:

- **No lineup recorded:** a match has a result but no lineup for that side.
- **No substitutions recorded:** a side listed substitutes but none came on, which usually means substitutions weren't entered.

A correction in Rugby Xplorer clears these gaps. They don't catch everything, so a count can be low with no flag: a side may have entered only some of its substitutions, or a substitution may name a player who can't be matched to exactly one listed substitute.

## How the data stays current

**Every night (06:00 UTC).** An automated job re-reads the current season and republishes the report:

1. It collects the current season's fixture lists.
2. It re-reads every match with a score this season, including earlier ones, so corrections made in Rugby Xplorer are picked up by the next run.
3. It saves each match's full record in an archive, then works out each club's participation and gaps from the archive.
4. It publishes the result with the page.

**Results before the current season are locked.** A season gets a last read as the next one begins, and is then kept as archived, so a later change to it in Rugby Xplorer doesn't appear here. Past seasons don't affect the current verdicts, and their own verdicts don't change once published.

Each update is all or nothing. If any request fails, the previous night's data stays in place, because a partial count could wrongly pass or fail a player. If the CMS answers but holds no details for a match, that isn't a failure, and the match shows as "No lineup recorded".

**Between nightly runs** the report doesn't change. Matches played, corrections made and fixtures added or rescheduled since the last run appear after the next one. The report shows when its data was last updated.

## The CSV report

The report can also read a Rugby Xplorer Match Participation Report for any club. It works out the club and its sides from the Team Name column, and applies the same rules. That report is the union's official count and carries USA IDs and registration dates, so its totals are taken as given, using Played + Substitute. Its rounds aren't linked to match reports.

## Reproducing a result

Every published number is a function of the archived Rugby Xplorer match records, the rules above and a timestamp. `bun run verify` recalculates all of the published data from the archive and fails on any difference, and the engine is published as the npm package `@zmnatz/usa-club-rugby-data` for use in other tools. The [step-by-step guide](https://github.com/zmnatz/usa-club-rugby-eligibility/blob/main/docs/reproducing.md) covers each stage from the CMS to a verdict.
